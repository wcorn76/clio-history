# Curriculum and source grounding

## User curriculum

The Chapter 5 uploaded teacher guides, workbook, source packet, and TEKS crosswalk were read from the supplied local attachments. Chapter 6 was retrieved from the current `26-27 Social Studies Curriculum` Drive folder on October 14, 2026 UTC (October 13 in Texas).

Chapter 6 retrieval included the START HERE guide, teacher guide Days 1–2, teacher guide Days 3–4, TEKS crosswalk, and primary source packet. The teacher guides and crosswalks governed scope; the older scope-and-sequence row was not used to override their explicit corrections.

The raw user source documents and their private Drive identifiers are deliberately excluded from the open-source package. This package contains original practice, public source links, and a record of curriculum alignment.

## Scope boundaries

The app does not claim comprehensive coverage of all standards taught across these chapters. It targets core chapter knowledge and selected associated skills. It does not score oral discussion, student-generated category design, constructed-response writing, or every geographic location. The people bank includes all fourteen current names in 8.4(B), plus John Paul Jones under 8.22(B). It avoids the inaccurate older count of eleven or thirteen people.

8.15(C) is represented only through colonial grievances and a bridge to institutional design; the Constitution and Bill of Rights remedies belong to Chapter 7. The Northwest Ordinance does not by itself complete 8.12(B)’s entire slavery-and-plantation standard. The app therefore primarily tags those tasks 8.6(A), rather than claiming the larger standard is completed.

## Labeling and historical precision

The data tables are hypothetical and say so. Visuals are original schematics, not historical maps or scale reproductions. Summaries are not placed in quotation marks as if contemporaries wrote them. The Northwest Ordinance’s fugitive clause is taught alongside its slavery restriction. Treaty boundaries are distinguished from actual control and Native consent. The Articles’ limited authority is distinguished from an absence of all authority.
