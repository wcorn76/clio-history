# Clio 0.3 beta: chapter choices, clearer language, and recall

## Changes

390 tasks: 206 in Chapter 5 and 184 in Chapter 6. The 124 additions include two forms for each of 36 vocabulary terms—recognizing a meaning and naming the word in a short example—plus 52 questions about people, dates, places, documents, and rules. These tasks intentionally support fluency and recall. They are not presented as newly rigorous analysis items merely because they have multiple choices.

Each chapter now offers Mixed practice, Strategy practice, and Quick recall & words. Strategy practice has twelve authored questions per chapter. Its chapter choice is explicit before the first question, and the scheduler remains within that chapter. Quick recall likewise stays in the chapter. The prominent mixed guided path can still span both chapters; it aims for two application questions for each retrieval question while both kinds remain available.

The strategy reading step asks “What do you need to find out?” Its options name the concrete issue in the actual question. For example: “Whether one state can stop this change to the Articles,” rather than “Apply an amendment rule to a disagreement among states.” The next steps say “Cross out one answer” and “Explain why.” Abstract staff-facing language is removed from the student instructions.

Regular-practice prompts, options, scenarios, hints, and explanations received a plain-language pass. The longest abstract prompts and all 24 strategy prompts received explicit rewrites. Historical terms remain where they are the learning target; quoted primary-source language is preserved. A protected rewrite layer applies changes to our own wording without rewriting quotations or breaking word endings. Existing task identities and answer keys are retained for the same content; older progress is not renumbered simply because wording became clearer.

## Five review perspectives

**Internal critic.** The chapter issue was a real routing defect in the intended experience, not a content problem. The global strategy entry is removed. Both chapter routes are exercised in tests, including repeated questions after the initial strategy set is exhausted. The recall cadence is also tested separately from question volume: it does not allow the enlarged retrieval bank to dominate normal mixed practice while application tasks remain available.

**History specialist.** Simpler language must not broaden a key or erase an exception. The revision preserves the Articles’ distinct voting rules, the land ordinances’ separate functions, the Northwest Ordinance’s territorial scope and fugitive clause, and the difference between treaty recognition and actual control. Vocabulary covers curriculum terms such as unalienable, consent, confederation, ratify, requisition, ordinance, and equal footing. The statehood population question asks what threshold the ordinance names; it is not a claim that population alone guarantees admission.

**Eighth grader.** The strategy choices now state the particular question the student needs to answer, rather than asking the student to classify an abstract cognitive operation. Direct fact and word questions provide shorter entry points and repeated practice. Correcting a mistake still requires a new answer and a reason. Recall hints initially direct thinking without dumping the answer, while repeated difficulty makes a worked explanation available. This is a reasoned language revision, not a measured reading-grade certification or a replacement for student observation.

**User perspective.** The three requested changes are implemented: chapter-scoped strategy, clearer language in both modes, and substantial drill practice alongside analysis. The new recall count is transparent. Repeating a concept through a definition and an example is intentional retrieval practice, not inflated claims of novel analytical thinking. Users can select quick recall when they specifically want drill, or keep the mixed path that emphasizes application.

**Central-office skeptic.** The bank remains inspectable and deterministic, with no additional collection of student data. Revision does not claim that readability has been validated with a representative group of eighth graders. Twenty-two automated checks cover supplied-key grading, all five formats, correction gates, strategy gates, both chapter routes, recall-only scope, application/recall cadence, quote protection, and standalone bundling. Tests use a document mock rather than a rendered browser. The GitHub/Vercel access limitation is unchanged; this update is the downloadable beta, not a claimed live deployment.

## Further review

The remaining useful pilot question is whether actual students understand the first strategy step without adult explanation. Also watch whether wrong-answer reasons teach a connection or merely cue a selection. Feedback from these observations should guide further wording and distractor revisions. Preserve curricular vocabulary while simplifying the surrounding explanation.
